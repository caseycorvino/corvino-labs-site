Where we learned it

Lavender, and ten years of being the person who had to make it work.

Corvino Labs did not start with a thesis about AI. It started with a decade of shipping software under the two constraints every company has: not enough people, and customers who notice immediately when something breaks.

Before

Small teams, real customers, no room for theatre

Casey finished New York University in three years and went straight to CTO of an early-stage startup automating fashion PR.

It set the pattern for everything after. A team too small to specialise, a budget too thin to buy your way out of a problem, and software that had to work on Monday morning.

Sorter

The problem underneath the problem

He led engineering on Sorter alongside Will Allred and William Ballance, applying personality and communication psychology to marketing.

The lesson that outlasted the company: the hard part of communication software is never generating the words. It is knowing what good looks like, and saying so before the message goes out.

2020

Sorter becomes Lavender

The three of them rebuilt it around one sharp job. Coach a salesperson on the email they are writing, in real time, while they can still change it.

Casey co-founded Lavender as CTO, owning product and engineering.

At scale

Lavender grew past 30,000 active users across Twilio, Clari, Sendoso, Demandbase, Segment, MasterClass and thousands of smaller teams.

It raised an $11M Series A led by top-tier investors including Norwest Venture Partners.

Casey stayed customer-facing the whole way, hyperfocused on the specific problem in front of the specific customer instead of the roadmap in the abstract.

Now

Corvino Labs

Being CTO was not just building the product. It meant getting the rest of the company to actually adopt AI and get 10x out of it, not just the engineering team.

Companies kept asking a version of the same question: we bought the AI, why is nothing different?

The answer was always the same three gaps, and we had already spent years closing them for ourselves. So we started a company to close them for other people’s teams.

What we built there

It was the product, not a pilot.

Lavender was the thing customers paid for, running every working day on their real email. Four decisions made that possible, and all four are load-bearing in what we install for you.

01

A coach in the window where the work happened

Lavender did not wait in a separate tab to be visited. It worked on the draft in front of the rep, at the only moment the advice was still worth anything: before they hit send.

AI that requires somebody to remember to go and use it gets used by the people who were already enthusiastic. That is the same three volunteers every time.

02

A scoring engine that had read more email than anyone

Every email scored against what the system had learned from millions of real ones, turned into specific fixes rather than a grade. That is Sorter’s inheritance, made operational.

Lavender was never a generative AI company. It was a communication intelligence company that happened to write text at the end.

03

A deliberate decision not to build a model

Casey adopted large language models early, well before it was fashionable, and made a deliberate call not to staff a research team to do it.

Lavender hired backend engineers to use the models that already existed. It saved millions and kept the product cheap enough to sell. It is why we will never try to sell you a model.

04

Everything that was not the model

Almost none of the difficulty was intelligence. It was access, plumbing, and the unglamorous integrations that had to hold while thirty thousand people worked on their own live data.

Running AI for that many people is a different discipline from demonstrating it once. That difference is most of what we sell.

“I was an early adopter of AI. Thousands of hours later, I know what actually works for a scaling org and what doesn’t. Now I’m putting it to work for you.”

Casey Corvino, co-founder & CTO, Lavender
