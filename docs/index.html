Where most AI initiatives fail

Everyone was promised 10x. Almost nobody is getting it.

Your team is getting something out of it — a faster email here, a decent first draft there. Useful, but nowhere near what you were sold. The tools are good enough now. The gap is that they aren't plugged into anything and nobody has told them how your company actually works.

01

It automates a job nobody actually does

Somebody picked the use case in a conference room, working from a description of the job instead of the job. Descriptions are wrong in a predictable direction: they keep the interesting part and drop the waiting, the re-keying, and the four systems the task crosses on its way to done.

How we do it differently

We spend the first week sitting with your people, watching the real work at the speed it actually happens. We come back with a ranked list and the hours behind each line, plus a second list of what to leave alone.

02

It can't reach your tools

It can talk about the work all day. It can't do the work, because it has no access to your CRM, your inbox, your billing system, or the internal tool your team built two years ago.

How we do it differently

We don't stop at the systems that ship with a connector. Your vertical software, your billing platform, the dashboard nobody else can integrate with. Where a vendor offers no API at all, we drive the browser signed in, clicking the same screens your team does.

03

Your best workflows live on one laptop

One person on your team is getting real leverage out of AI. They spent a hundred hours getting there. Nobody else has any of it.

How we do it differently

We get those hundred hours out of their head and write them down as skills anyone can run: named, version-controlled, reviewable. A clever paragraph in one person's chat history can't be handed to anybody.

04

Nobody trusts it with the real work

With no rules about what it can and can't touch, the work that actually matters never gets handed over. So the value stays small and the pilot quietly dies.

How we do it differently

Your managers set the limits on purpose: spend caps, who it may contact, brand voice, what it must never guess at. Each one is a check in the path of the call rather than a polite request. Rules aren't the brake. They're the permission slip.

05

It only ever reached the volunteers

The pilot went to the three people who put their hands up, and it worked for them. That proves motivated people can get value out of it, which was never the question. The median person on the team never got it, and everybody hired since started at zero.

How we do it differently

The whole team gets the same connections, skills and rules installed. The ten-year veteran, the person who has never opened an AI tool, and the hire who starts next month, because the setup is part of onboarding.

06

Everything you built is stuck inside one vendor

The connections, the workflows, the rules all live in one product's account, in one product's format. Harnesses have been lasting about eighteen months. When this one gets replaced, you do the whole thing again.

How we do it differently

We build that layer so you own it, separate from whatever tool runs it today. Switch AI tools next year and the connections, skills and rules come with you. Nothing here depends on us being in the room either.

The plan

Five steps. It starts with watching.

Step 1

We shadow your team

Before we build anything, we sit with your people and watch the real work happen — which tools they open, where they sit waiting on somebody else, what they rebuild by hand every week. This is the step most AI projects skip, and it's why most of them automate the wrong things.

Step 2

Connect it to everything you use

Slack, Gmail, Drive, your CRM, your billing system, the internal dashboard nobody else can integrate with. Agentic work breaks if even one tool is missing, so we don't stop until all of them are in.

Step 3

Teach it how your team does the work

The workflows we watched in step one — the way you write a proposal, close a month, escalate a ticket — become skills anyone on the team can run.

Step 4

Set the rules it can never break

Managers decide what can be delegated and what can't. Brand voice, spend limits, who it's allowed to email, what it must never guess at. Enforced, not suggested.

Step 5

Roll it out to everyone

New hire on day one, ten-year veteran, whole department — everybody gets the same connections, skills, and rules installed. Not a pilot with three volunteers.

Customer story

EPL Media: from constrained to scale.

Background

EPL Media is an out-of-home advertising company specializing in media planning and buying. They help their clients get more out of billboard and poster campaigns.

Problem

EPL hit a classic constraint: hire more people to scale revenue, or keep revenue flat. Out-of-home delivery is operationally taxing. They knew AI could help. They didn't know how much.

How we helped

We shadowed the business before we built anything. That surfaced several workflows that were unnecessarily manual: client delivery, back office admin, reporting, and resource management.

Step 01

Connect all of their systems

The plumbing first. Every system the work already ran through, wired into one place, so the AI could reach the real data instead of talking about it.

Their finances ran through QuickBooks, which had no connector worth using. We built a custom QuickBooks MCP so the back office was reachable like anything else.

Step 02

Build the AI onto the manual work

With the systems reachable, we built on the four areas the shadowing surfaced: client delivery, back office admin, reporting, and resource management. We took the manual steps out of each one.

Planning needed a tool that did not exist, so we built one. Telos is their internal out-of-home planning system, and the logistics side of the business now runs on it.

Step 03

They're building without us

The step that matters. EPL's own team now extends the system themselves. New workflows go in without us in the room.
